Inspiration — three ways in

Three entry points to the same recommendation engine, presented as greyscale wireframes. Each one leads to the same results page, so the difference being tested is purely how the customer gives us their brief.

Entry point 1

Guided brief

A structured panel of questions. Nothing to type unless you want to.
  • Lowest effort — every field optional
  • Customer sees the full shape of what we ask
  • Weakest at anything we did not think to ask
Open concept 1
Entry point 2

Prompt, with help on demand

An open prompt with suggestions — and the concept 1 questions behind a “Need help?” button.
  • Fastest for anyone who knows what they want
  • “Need help?” builds the prompt for anyone who doesn't
  • Customer can edit the generated prompt before sending
Open concept 2
Entry point 3

Conversation

One text box that asks up to three follow-ups, adapting to what you already said.
  • Feels like being served, not filling a form
  • Only asks what the opening message missed
  • Longest path to a result — “just show me” at every step
Open concept 3

What all three lead to

Entry point → Results: best match ↔ Premium ↔ Off the beaten track → Edit brief (side panel) → Re-picked
  • Three destinations the customer toggles between, re-scored live against the brief.
  • Three flight options and six hotels per destination, split into lower, middle and higher budget tiers, with an indicative trip total that reprices as you change either.
  • “Nudge it” chips and a conversational Edit brief side panel, both of which redo the shortlist.
  • Jump straight to the results page with an empty brief to see the fallback behaviour.

Greyscale throughout, with a single accent reserved for the primary action — matching the agreed wireframe treatment. All destinations, flights, hotels and prices are indicative placeholders for demonstration only.
